Paolo Marzi.

About me

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher in Political Science at the University of Siena, where I currently work on the Horizon Europe project REDIRECT (The Representative Disconnect) and serve as its Communication and Dissemination Manager. Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Turin, working on the Horizon 2020 project EUComMeet.

My research interests lie at the intersection of political representation, political elites, political parties, and European politics. I am particularly interested in the relationship between citizens and their elected representatives, examining how closely political elites reflect public preferences, how citizens understand and evaluate political representation, and how political parties and institutions respond to changing political contexts. My research also addresses Euroscepticism, populism, parliamentary careers, and political behaviour within the European Union.

Methodologically, my work is primarily quantitative and comparative. I rely on cross-national surveys, mass–elite datasets, longitudinal analyses, and survey experiments to investigate political attitudes, representative behaviour, and institutional dynamics. My research has been published in journals including European Political Science Review, European Union Politics, Italian Political Science Review, Acta Politica, Politics and Governance, and The Journal of Legislative Studies.

02 / Research

Research interests.

My research connects the study of democratic representation with parliamentary elites, European political parties and institutions.

Representation, elites & European politics.

I study who is represented, how political representatives make decisions, and how parties and institutions respond to political change. I address these questions through comparative surveys, mass–elite data, quantitative analysis and experiments, with a particular focus on European politics and integration.

Whom do you serve? A comparative analysis of congruence between party representatives, voters, and economic elites (2007–2017)

Paolo Marzi & Michele Scotto di Vettimo

European Political Science Review · 18(3), 410–428

Using mass–elite surveys from 16 European countries (2007–2017) and Earth Mover’s Distance, this article compares party representatives’ alignment with voters and economic elites. Italy, the EU-9, and the double-sided gap: A longitudinal analysis of mass–elite congruence on European integration (1979–2016)

Paolo Marzi & Andrea Pareschi

Italian Political Science Review · 55(2), 121–137

Based on eight survey waves spanning 1979–2016, this study identifies a “double-sided gap”: pro-European elites are generally more supportive of integration than their voters, while Eurosceptic elites are often more opposed. Italy follows a different, more congruent pattern.

More equal than different? Gender and legislative turnover in the European Parliament and west European legislatures

Athanassios Gouglas, Paolo Marzi, Bart Maddens, Marleen Brans & Luca Verzichelli

European Union Politics · 26(1), 167–184

Combining historical data on the European Parliament and eight Western European national legislatures, this study examines gender differences in incumbent exit rates. Men are more likely than women to exit after European Parliament elections, whereas the gender gap is not significant for national legislatures.
